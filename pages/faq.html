ABOUT THIS FAQ

Understanding How GRAITE Works

GRAITE (Global Research in AI Technologies and Engineering) envisions a global, multidisciplinary institution connecting researchers, scientists, engineers, educators, institutions, industry professionals and public-interest contributors.

These questions explain how GRAITE intends to operate globally, establish Country Chapters, develop governance, undertake research and engineering projects, and create opportunities for participation.

The structures and processes described here represent GRAITE's proposed institutional framework. Formal appointments, elections, eligibility requirements and decision-making procedures will be governed by approved institutional documents and applicable laws.

01 / THE ORGANISATION

About GRAITE and Its Global Mission

Understanding the organisation, its purpose and its global vision.

What is GRAITE?

GRAITE stands for Global Research in AI Technologies and Engineering. It is envisioned as a global research and innovation organisation dedicated to advancing artificial intelligence through scientific research, engineering, education, responsible development and real-world applications.

What is the primary mission of GRAITE?

GRAITE's mission is to advance safe, responsible and transformative AI for the benefit of humanity. It seeks to connect scientific discovery with practical engineering, encourage international collaboration and promote AI that contributes positively to society.

Is GRAITE a research organisation, a professional association or an innovation network?

GRAITE is intended to bring together elements of all three. It envisions a global institutional framework for research, professional collaboration, education, technology development, responsible AI and public-interest innovation.

Why does GRAITE need a global structure?

AI research and its implications extend beyond national boundaries. A global structure can enable researchers and institutions from different countries to collaborate, exchange knowledge, address shared challenges and develop solutions that can be adapted to local needs.

Does GRAITE focus only on artificial intelligence?

AI is its central focus, but its work may extend into associated disciplines such as computer science, engineering, mathematics, healthcare, education, environmental science, public policy, social sciences, robotics and other fields where AI can create meaningful impact.

What makes GRAITE different from a conventional technology company?

GRAITE's envisioned institutional purpose is broader than developing or selling technology. It seeks to connect research, scientific knowledge, responsible engineering, education, governance and public-interest applications. Commercial and industry collaborations may support its mission, but they do not replace its research and public-interest objectives.

Who can become part of GRAITE?

Researchers, scientists, engineers, academicians, students, technology professionals, entrepreneurs, institutional representatives, policy experts and individuals interested in responsible AI may express interest in participating, subject to applicable membership and participation criteria.

02 / GLOBAL OPERATIONS

How GRAITE Works Globally

The relationship between the global institution and its international network.

How will GRAITE operate across different countries?

GRAITE envisions a global institutional structure supported by Country Chapters. The global organisation will provide overall mission direction, institutional policies, research priorities and common governance standards. Country Chapters will facilitate national-level research, collaborations, programmes and engagement.

Will GRAITE have a central global governing body?

Yes. The proposed structure includes a Global Governing Board or Board of Trustees responsible for protecting the institution's mission, providing strategic oversight, approving key institutional policies and ensuring accountability.

The precise legal form, composition and authority of this body will be established through GRAITE's governing documents.

What will the global organisation be responsible for?

The global organisation is envisaged to coordinate:

  • Overall institutional vision, mission and strategy.
  • Global governance policies and institutional standards.
  • International research priorities and collaborative programmes.
  • Global partnerships with universities, research institutions, industry and public organisations.
  • Research integrity, AI safety and responsible technology principles.
  • Global fellowships, scholarships and capacity-building initiatives.
  • Knowledge exchange, publications, conferences and international events.
  • Monitoring institutional performance and impact.
Will every country follow the same research priorities?

Country Chapters will operate within GRAITE's common mission and institutional framework. However, they may identify national and regional research priorities based on local challenges, capabilities, policy environments and opportunities.

Can researchers from different countries work together?

Yes. International collaboration is a central part of GRAITE's vision. Researchers and project teams may collaborate across Country Chapters, disciplines and institutions, subject to project approvals, applicable agreements and research governance requirements.

How will the global organisation ensure consistency across countries?

Common institutional policies, research standards, governance principles, reporting requirements and accountability mechanisms are envisaged to provide consistency. Country Chapters will adapt implementation to their national context while respecting the global framework.

03 / NATIONAL NETWORK

Country Chapters and National Operations

How GRAITE establishes, governs and develops its presence in individual countries.

What is a GRAITE Country Chapter?

A Country Chapter is the proposed national-level institutional structure through which GRAITE's mission, research activities, partnerships and programmes can be developed within a particular country.

Why does GRAITE need Country Chapters?

Country Chapters can help GRAITE build local research communities, connect with universities and institutions, identify national challenges, develop partnerships and enable participation by local researchers and professionals.

Who will lead a Country Chapter?

Each Country Chapter is envisaged to be led by a Country Chapter Governance Board, headed by a Country Chapter Governance Board Chief.

The Board will provide national-level governance, strategic direction and oversight within the authority delegated to the Country Chapter under GRAITE's global framework.

Will Country Chapters have their own governance structure?

Yes. The proposed national structure may include:

  • Country Chapter Governance Board.
  • Country Chapter Governance Board Chief.
  • National Research and Programme Leadership.
  • Research committees and programme coordinators.
  • National researchers, fellows and members.
  • Advisory groups and subject-matter experts.

The exact structure may vary according to the scale, legal status and activities of the Country Chapter.

Will Country Chapters be independent organisations?

Country Chapters are intended to function as part of the wider GRAITE institutional network. Their legal status, registration requirements, financial arrangements and operational authority will depend on the approved global framework and the laws of the respective country.

A Country Chapter should not assume independent legal or financial authority unless such authority has been formally established.

Can a Country Chapter develop its own research projects?

Yes. Country Chapters may identify, propose and coordinate research and innovation projects relevant to their national context. Projects involving global resources, international partnerships or institutional commitments will require the appropriate approvals.

Can a Country Chapter partner with local universities, companies or government organisations?

Country Chapters may develop partnerships with universities, academic institutions, research centres, industry, government bodies and non-profit organisations, subject to GRAITE's partnership policies and delegated approval authority.

Can one country collaborate with another Country Chapter?

Yes. Country Chapters may jointly develop research programmes, exchange experts, organise events and undertake international projects through agreed coordination arrangements.

How can someone help establish a GRAITE Country Chapter?

Interested individuals and institutions may express their interest by submitting their professional background, institutional association, proposed national activities and potential contribution.

The proposal may be evaluated on research capacity, institutional alignment, leadership capability, governance readiness and the ability to support GRAITE's mission.

Expression of interest does not automatically establish or authorise a Country Chapter. Formal recognition will require approval through the designated institutional process.

Can a country have more than one GRAITE Chapter?

The proposed model is based on a recognised national Country Chapter. Regional, city-level, university-based or specialised research units may be considered as subordinate or affiliated structures where justified and formally approved.

04 / LEADERSHIP & ACCOUNTABILITY

Governance, Leadership and Decision-Making

How institutional leadership, councils, appointments and member voting are envisaged.

What is the purpose of GRAITE's governance system?

Governance exists to protect the institution's mission, ensure responsible leadership, maintain research integrity, manage institutional resources and provide transparency and accountability.

It is intended to ensure that GRAITE operates as an institution rather than as an informal network dependent on individual personalities.

What are the main governance bodies envisaged by GRAITE?
  • Global Governing Board / Board of Trustees: Institutional direction, mission protection and strategic oversight.
  • Global Advisory Council: Independent and multidisciplinary expert advice.
  • Executive Director: Institutional execution and operational management.
  • Global Research and Programme Leadership: Coordination of research, programmes and institutional initiatives.
  • Independent Oversight Committees: Scientific review, AI safety and ethics, audit, finance and research integrity.

Country Chapters will have their corresponding national governance arrangements.

What is the Global Advisory Council?

The Global Advisory Council is envisaged as a multidisciplinary advisory body comprising experienced researchers, scientists, engineers, academicians, industry experts, AI safety specialists, policy professionals and other distinguished contributors.

Its role is to advise on research directions, scientific priorities, international collaboration, institutional development and emerging AI challenges.

Is the Advisory Council the same as the Governing Board?

No. The Governing Board is responsible for institutional governance and oversight within its approved authority. The Advisory Council provides expertise, recommendations and strategic advice.

An advisory position does not automatically carry executive, financial or legal decision-making authority.

How can someone become part of the Global Advisory Council?

Individuals may express interest or be considered through nomination and institutional identification.

Potential considerations include:

  • Demonstrated expertise in a relevant discipline.
  • Research, engineering, academic or professional contributions.
  • Experience in leadership, governance or institutional development.
  • Commitment to responsible AI and GRAITE's mission.
  • Ability to contribute to the Council's work and responsibilities.
  • Disclosure and management of potential conflicts of interest.

Appointments will require review and approval by the competent authority under GRAITE's approved governance framework.

How can someone become part of a Country Chapter Governance Board?

Interested and eligible individuals may be nominated, invited or considered through the national governance process, subject to the applicable global and country-level rules.

Relevant factors may include professional experience, national research contribution, institutional leadership, integrity, commitment and capacity to fulfil governance responsibilities.

Can an ordinary GRAITE member become a governance leader?

Membership may provide a pathway to participate in institutional activities and, where eligibility requirements are met, to be considered for governance responsibilities.

However, membership alone does not create an automatic right to a governance appointment, leadership position or institutional authority.

How will governance positions be filled — appointment, nomination or election?

GRAITE's proposed governance model may combine nomination, eligibility review, appointment and member voting, depending on the nature of the position.

The final method for each office will need to be defined in the constitution and election rules. Not every governance position necessarily needs to be elected.

Will GRAITE members have voting rights?

Voting rights are intended to be defined by membership category, eligibility, institutional level and the applicable governing documents.

Where elections or member resolutions are provided for, eligible members may participate according to the relevant voting rules. Membership alone should not be interpreted as an automatic voting entitlement.

How might members elect or vote for governance representatives?

A formal election framework may define:

  • Who is eligible to vote.
  • Which membership categories have voting rights.
  • Minimum membership duration, if applicable.
  • Candidate eligibility and nomination requirements.
  • Electoral constituencies and representation.
  • Voting method, timelines and verification.
  • Quorum and result declaration.
  • Election oversight and dispute resolution.

For example, national-level eligible members may participate in elections for designated Country Chapter positions, while eligible global members may participate in elections for designated global positions, if the approved constitution provides for them.

Will every member be allowed to vote on every decision?

Not necessarily. Institutional decisions may be divided among the Governing Board, executive leadership, committees and members according to their delegated authority.

Members may have voting rights on specified elections or resolutions, but routine operational, research and financial decisions will be handled by the designated authorities.

Can a governance position be removed or changed?

The governing documents are expected to define terms of office, resignation, removal, suspension, replacement, accountability and conflict-of-interest procedures.

No governance position should be treated as permanent or beyond institutional accountability.

How will GRAITE prevent conflicts of interest?

GRAITE intends to establish conflict-of-interest disclosure and management requirements. Individuals involved in governance, research review, funding or procurement may be required to disclose relevant interests and abstain from decisions where necessary.

How will institutional decisions be made transparent?

The proposed framework includes documented decisions, defined responsibilities, appropriate financial reporting, periodic reviews, research evaluation and institutional accountability.

Information will be shared according to applicable confidentiality, privacy and legal requirements.

05 / RESEARCH & INNOVATION

Research, Projects and Innovation

How ideas become research programmes, engineering projects and real-world solutions.

What types of projects will GRAITE undertake?

GRAITE envisions projects across a broad range of AI research and engineering areas, including:

  • Fundamental and applied AI research.
  • AI safety, alignment and responsible development.
  • Generative AI and knowledge systems.
  • Diagnostic and predictive intelligence.
  • Decision intelligence and agentic AI.
  • Robotics, automation and intelligent systems.
  • Healthcare and scientific research.
  • Education and learning technologies.
  • Agriculture, environment and sustainability.
  • Industry, infrastructure and public-interest applications.
  • AI governance, policy and societal impact.
How does GRAITE identify new projects?

Projects may originate from research teams, Country Chapters, institutional partners, advisory experts, industry collaborations, public-interest challenges or internally identified research priorities.

Proposals may be evaluated according to scientific merit, relevance, feasibility, expected impact, available resources, safety and alignment with GRAITE's mission.

Who can propose a project?

Researchers, members, fellows, experts, Country Chapters, universities, research institutions, industry partners and other eligible collaborators may propose projects through the applicable institutional process.

A proposal does not automatically become an approved GRAITE project.

How does a project move from an idea to implementation?
  1. Idea and Problem Identification: Define the problem, opportunity or research question.
  2. Proposal Development: Prepare objectives, methodology, expected outcomes and resource requirements.
  3. Scientific and Technical Review: Evaluate feasibility, research quality and technical approach.
  4. Safety and Ethics Assessment: Examine risks, responsible AI considerations, privacy and potential societal impact.
  5. Approval and Resource Allocation: Obtain the required institutional approvals and funding commitments.
  6. Project Team Formation: Identify researchers, engineers, institutions and collaborators.
  7. Execution and Monitoring: Conduct the work with milestones, documentation and oversight.
  8. Validation and Evaluation: Assess scientific validity, technical performance and real-world suitability.
  9. Publication or Deployment: Publish, share, transfer or deploy outputs under approved agreements.
  10. Impact Assessment: Evaluate outcomes, lessons learned and opportunities for further development.
Who owns a GRAITE project?

Project ownership, intellectual property, data rights, software rights, publications and commercialisation arrangements will depend on the project structure and written agreements.

These matters should be defined before work begins, particularly where multiple researchers, institutions, funders or commercial partners are involved.

Participation in a project does not automatically transfer an individual's pre-existing intellectual property to GRAITE.

Can projects be undertaken jointly with external institutions?

Yes. GRAITE may collaborate with universities, research laboratories, hospitals, industry, government bodies, non-profit organisations and international institutions.

The roles, responsibilities, funding, intellectual property, publication rights, data access and accountability arrangements should be documented in appropriate agreements.

Will GRAITE fund its own research projects?

GRAITE may seek to support research through grants, institutional contributions, donations, sponsorships, research partnerships and other lawful funding arrangements.

Whether a particular project receives financial support will depend on available resources, approvals, funding conditions and institutional priorities.

Can a project be commercially developed?

Yes, where appropriate. GRAITE envisions a pathway from research to engineering, validation, product development, licensing, technology transfer and responsible commercialisation.

Commercial arrangements should be transparent, properly authorised and consistent with the institution's mission and applicable agreements.

Will every GRAITE project result in a product?

No. Some projects may produce scientific knowledge, publications, datasets, methodologies, open-source tools, prototypes, policy recommendations or validated technologies rather than commercial products.

Research value is not measured only by commercialisation.

How will GRAITE evaluate project success?
  • Scientific and technical quality.
  • Achievement of defined objectives.
  • Reliability, safety and validation.
  • Reproducibility and research integrity.
  • Practical usefulness and adoption.
  • Social, economic or environmental impact.
  • Knowledge creation and capacity building.
  • Long-term sustainability.
Can a project be stopped if it is not producing expected results?

Yes. Projects should be subject to periodic review. If a project becomes scientifically invalid, technically infeasible, unsafe, financially unsustainable or inconsistent with approved objectives, it may be revised, paused or discontinued through the appropriate review process.

Opportunities for researchers, engineers, students, experts and collaborators.

Some opportunities may require membership or a formal institutional association.

Can students and early-career researchers participate?

Yes. GRAITE envisions opportunities for students, doctoral researchers and early-career professionals through research teams, fellowships, mentorship, training, collaborative projects and other programmes, subject to availability and selection requirements.

Can I contribute remotely from another country?

Depending on the project, remote participation and international collaboration may be possible. The work arrangement will depend on research requirements, data access, applicable laws, institutional agreements and project policies.

Can I contribute without receiving payment?

Yes. Voluntary knowledge sharing, mentoring, peer review and other voluntary contributions may be possible where agreed.

However, voluntary participation is not a condition for all project roles. Any paid position, consultancy, grant-funded role, reimbursement or other financial arrangement must be separately approved and documented.

Will I receive a certificate for participating in a project?

Certificates, acknowledgements or formal recognition may be provided where a programme or project has an approved recognition policy.

Participation alone does not guarantee a certificate, academic credit, professional qualification or employment reference.

Can I lead a research project?

Individuals with relevant expertise may propose or be considered for project leadership roles. Appointment will depend on scientific capability, project requirements, institutional approval, available resources and defined responsibilities.

Can I bring my own research team?

How will research contributions be recognised?

Contributions may be recognised through authorship, acknowledgements, technical credits, project roles or other approved mechanisms, in accordance with research integrity standards and applicable agreements.

Recognition should reflect actual contributions rather than membership status or designation alone.

07 / MEMBERSHIP & FELLOWSHIPS

Membership, Fellowships and Professional Participation

Understanding membership categories, fellowships and the responsibilities of association.

What is GRAITE membership?

Membership is intended to provide a voluntary association with GRAITE's research and institutional community. It may provide opportunities to engage in knowledge sharing, events, research discussions, collaboration and approved institutional activities.

Is GRAITE membership free?

The membership framework may provide free voluntary membership, subject to the applicable membership policy. Any separate paid programme, event, training, service or funded opportunity will have its own clearly stated terms.

Does membership mean I am employed by GRAITE?

No. Membership does not create an employment relationship, consultancy, partnership, agency, salary entitlement or professional appointment.

Any formal paid engagement must be established through a separate written agreement.

Does membership give me authority to represent GRAITE?

No. Members cannot automatically represent GRAITE, make institutional commitments, enter into agreements, collect funds or use institutional branding in an official capacity.

Such authority must be specifically granted in writing.

What is the difference between a Member and a Council participant?

A Member is part of the wider GRAITE community. A Council participant or Council member may hold a designated advisory or governance role, depending on the Council's nature and approved appointment process.

Applying for Council participation does not automatically confer a Council position.

How can I become a GRAITE Fellow?

GRAITE envisions different fellowship pathways, potentially including:

  • Research Fellows.
  • Innovation Fellows.
  • AI Fellows.
  • AI Safety and Governance Fellows.
  • Public Interest Technology Fellows.

Eligibility, selection, tenure, responsibilities and benefits will be defined separately for each fellowship programme when established.

Are fellowships paid?

Not necessarily. Fellowship arrangements may be voluntary, grant-supported, stipend-based or otherwise funded, depending on the programme and available resources. The terms of each fellowship will be specified in its official announcement.

Will GRAITE offer scholarships and research grants?

GRAITE envisions potential programmes such as PhD scholarships, scientist grants, early-career research grants, AI safety research grants and rural innovation support.

These are programme possibilities, not automatic membership benefits. Their availability, eligibility, funding and selection processes will be announced separately when established.

Can professionals from industry participate?

Yes. Industry professionals can contribute as researchers, engineers, mentors, technical experts, institutional partners, project collaborators or advisors, subject to relevant role requirements and conflict-of-interest policies.

Can an institution become associated with GRAITE?

Universities, research centres, companies, laboratories, non-profit organisations and other institutions may explore institutional collaboration or association.

The nature of the relationship, use of names and logos, project responsibilities and any financial arrangements will require formal approval and documentation.

09 / RESPONSIBLE AI

AI Safety, Ethics and Research Integrity

How GRAITE approaches responsible research and technology development.

Why is AI safety important to GRAITE?

AI systems can influence individuals, institutions, economies and society. GRAITE considers safety, reliability, transparency, accountability and human well-being essential considerations in AI research and engineering.

Will GRAITE develop AI safety standards?

GRAITE intends to contribute to responsible AI research, evaluation methods, governance frameworks, risk assessment and policy dialogue. Any formal standards or guidelines will require appropriate scientific review and institutional approval.

How will GRAITE handle unethical or unsafe research?

Research proposals and projects may be subject to scientific, ethical, safety and legal review. Activities that present unacceptable risks or violate approved policies may be restricted, revised, suspended or discontinued.

How will GRAITE protect research integrity?

GRAITE envisages policies covering research methodology, data integrity, authorship, plagiarism, conflicts of interest, responsible publication and transparent reporting.

Can members report misconduct or misuse of GRAITE's identity?

Yes. GRAITE intends to provide appropriate channels for reporting suspected fraud, impersonation, unauthorised representation, research misconduct, ethical concerns and misuse of its name or identity.

Reports should be made through official communication channels and handled according to applicable procedures.

Will GRAITE support independent review?

Independent scientific review, AI safety and ethics oversight, audit and financial review, and impact evaluation are part of the proposed institutional accountability framework.

The actual committees, independence safeguards and review procedures will be established through approved policies.
